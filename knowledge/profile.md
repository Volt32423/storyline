# Grandpa Frank: profile

> **Fictional.** Frank, his family and every story below are made up for this demo. None of it is about a real person.

## Who he is

- **Name:** Frank Novak. Everyone calls him Frank, and his grandson calls him Grandpa.
- **Age:** 84, born in 1942
- **Lives in:** Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, in the same brick house since 1965
- **Grew up:** in a small mill town just outside Pittsburgh, the oldest of three kids
- **Work:** worked at a steel mill from 1960 to 1974, then delivered mail for the Post Office until he retired in 2004
- **Late wife:** June. They met in 1961 and married in 1963. She passed away in 2019. Speak about her warmly, and follow his lead.
- **Family:**
  - Walt, his younger brother, who lives in Ohio
  - Linda, his daughter
  - Sam, his grandson, who set up Storyline
- **Loves:** Pirates baseball, fishing, June's pierogies, big band records, and a good joke
- **Memory:** his memory is fading. Recent details slip first, and older stories are often clearer. He can lose his place mid-story, and he may tell a story slightly differently each time. That's fine.

## Stories Frank has already told

### Rowboats at Pine Lake

- **People:** Walt (his younger brother)
- **Places:** Pine Lake
- **Summary:** The summer I was fourteen, Walt and I saved up to rent a rowboat at Pine Lake. It cost 50 cents an hour, which was a lot of money to us. Walt dropped an oar halfway out, and we had to paddle back with our hands while the man on the dock laughed at us. We went back every Saturday that summer anyway.

### Meeting June at the roller rink

- **People:** June (his late wife)
- **Places:** the roller rink on Fifth Street
- **Summary:** I met June at the roller rink on a Saturday night in 1961. She was a much better skater than me. I fell right in front of her, and she helped me up and said I skated like a mailbox. We were married two years later, and she never let me forget that line.

### The snowstorm on the mail route

- **People:** Mrs. Kowalski (a neighbor on his route)
- **Places:** his mail route on Maple Hill
- **Summary:** One winter in the late seventies we had a snowstorm so bad the trucks couldn't get up Maple Hill. I carried the mail up on foot because people were waiting on their checks. Mrs. Kowalski met me at her door with a mug of hot cocoa and made me sit down to drink it. After that, she had cocoa ready for me every time it snowed.

## Gentle reminders for the AI

- Offer memories. Never quiz him.
- If he mixes up details, go with his version.
- If he gets quiet or upset, reassure him and move to a happy topic like Pine Lake or the roller rink.
- Give no medical advice. If he mentions pain, a fall or feeling unwell, respond kindly and let the summary flag it for the family.
